# Quantum computing and AI, October 6, 2026

## Short version

- Quantum computing and AI meet today mainly in one direction: AI helps quantum computers (decoding errors, calibrating hardware). That work is real, shipping and partly open-weight.
- Quantum computing for AI is unproven on ordinary business data. One April 2026 theory preprint claims a provable advantage but needs fewer than 60 logical qubits that nobody has built.
- Timing is disputed. A survey of 26 experts puts a code-breaking quantum computer at 28 to 49 percent within 10 years. A well-known former skeptic says 2029 "ought to be possible".
- The practical deadline is cryptography: Google and Cloudflare target 2029 for migrating to post-quantum cryptography, with US government dates of 2030 and 2035 proposed.
- Most hardware-milestone detail comes from aggregators, so several numbers are only `likely`.

Confidence words: `verified`, `likely`, `not verified`. See the [README](README.md). Each source is shown as ([label](link), date).

## 1. AI for quantum: real and shipping

- **NVIDIA Ising (Apr 14, 2026).** Open models on Hugging Face for quantum calibration and error-correction decoding. Ising Calibration is a 35B-parameter vision-language model built on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (about 35B total, 3B active). Ising Decoding is two small 3D-CNN pre-decoders (about 0.9M and 1.8M parameters). Correction to the original claim: head-to-head accuracy gains over the PyMatching decoder are 1.11 to 1.53 times (the "Fast" model is 2.5 times faster with 1.11 times the accuracy; the "Accurate" model 2.25 times faster with 1.53 times the accuracy). The "3 times" figure is an extrapolated logical-error-rate gain at code distance 31 for a model trained at distance 13. Performance claims are NVIDIA's own. `verified` ([NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/?p=115554), 2026-04-14; [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Ising-Calibration-1), 2026-04-14).
- **Ising license.** The weights use the NVIDIA Open Model License, not Apache-2.0. Only the training framework, the calibration workflow and the cookbooks on GitHub are Apache-2.0. Anyone building commercial products on fine-tuned Ising weights should read NVIDIA's license. `verified` (same model card).
- **AlphaQubit 2 (Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI).** A neural decoder reaching real-time speed (under 1 microsecond per cycle) on commercial accelerators. It decodes the surface code to distance 11 and the color code to distance 9, tested on data from the 105-qubit Willow chip. The paper appeared Dec 8, 2025 (version 2 on Mar 11, 2026). Only the abstract was read. `verified` ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07737), 2025-12-08).
- **Google used reinforcement learning for error-correction calibration** (Nature, covered Jul 20, 2026): 3.5 times better logical-error stability and about 20 percent lower logical error rate. A Sep 22, 2026 arXiv item on this theme is a PhD thesis showing modest gains on noisy hardware and no quantum-speedup claim for machine learning. `verified` ([Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/07/20/google-uses-ai-reinforcement-learning-for-quantum-error-correction/5275023), 2026-07-20; [arXiv thesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25641), 2026-09-22).
- **NVIDIA's quantum-GPU stack.** NVQLink (Oct 2025), cudaq-realtime (Mar 16, 2026; measured round-trip latency about 2.8 to 9.7 microseconds) and CUDA-Q Logical (Sep 14, 2026; open source, partners include Google, IBM, Quantinuum, IonQ and others). NVIDIA says Fermilab cut fault-tolerant algorithm development from five months to three weeks. Quantum Machines "ran a demo" over NVQLink in Israel with no further detail. A 67-microsecond decoder figure for Quantinuum is `not verified`. NVQLink's launch date and adopter counts were not confirmed. `verified` for the first three ([NVIDIA press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Expands-Open-Source-CUDA-Q-Platform-for-Fault-Tolerant-Quantum-Computing/default.aspx), 2026-09-14; [cudaq-realtime](https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/blogs/blog/2026/03/16/launching-cudaq-realtime/), 2026-03-16).
- **QuEra** announced it deploys AI to automate a critical quantum-computer subsystem (Aug 27, 2026) and an HPE partnership (Sep 2026). Headlines only were read. `likely` ([QuEra news](https://www.quera.com/news), 2026-09-22).
- **Hybrid quantum-supercomputing centers** are mostly testbeds with small quantum processors. RIKEN launched the ROQUO supercomputer (19.8 petaflops FP64) on Jun 23, 2026 for a quantum-HPC hybrid project, with qubit systems undisclosed (`verified`, [Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/06/23/riken-launches-roquo-quantum-hpc-supercomputer/), 2026-06-23). Jülich's DGX Quantum deployment (Sep 2025, a 5-qubit processor, under 4 microseconds) was not checked (`not verified`, [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/julich-supercomputing-centre-deploys-nvidia-dgx-quantum-system-with-arque-and-quantum-machines/), 2025-09).

## 2. Quantum for AI: not shown on ordinary data

- A May 29, 2026 review says quantum machine learning has demonstrated advantage only on quantum data and constructed problems. No accepted advantage exists on ordinary classical business data. Views cited: Ewin Tang's "dequantization" results erased several celebrated speedups; Scott Aaronson's "Read the Fine Print" flags the data-loading trap; Maria Schuld thinks something real is worth chasing. The source is a single secondary review. `verified` for the statement ([postquantum.com review](https://postquantum.com/quantum-ai/quantum-machine-learning-reality/), 2026-05-29).
- **Barren plateaus.** Cerezo and colleagues ask whether provably avoiding barren plateaus (a training problem for quantum circuits) implies the circuit is classically simulable (Nature Communications 16, 7907, 2025). If so, trainable circuits may not beat classical methods. `verified` that the paper exists ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09121), 2025-08-25). Wider expert consensus was not checked.
- **The strongest counter-claim** is a theory preprint (Apr 8, 2026) by Zhao, Zlokapa, Neven, Babbush, Preskill, McClean and Huang. It claims a provable exponential advantage for large-scale classification and dimension reduction on massive classical data, using "quantum oracle sketching" to sidestep data loading, with 4 to 6 orders of magnitude size reduction and fewer than 60 logical qubits. It has no hardware and no peer review. A second version went up on Oct 1, 2026 and its changes were not reviewed. No independent rebuttal was found. Treat it as a serious claim to watch, not a result. `verified` ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07639), 2026-10-01).
- Marketing anything as "quantum AI" is not supported by the evidence above. (General reading of the review, not a separate source.) `likely`.

## 4. When might quantum break encryption?

Resource estimates for breaking today's public-key cryptography have collapsed, but they describe designs, not built machines. Today's best verified logical-qubit counts are in the single digits to tens.

|---|---|---|

| A neutral-atom design needs about 500,000 | Xue and Covey ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03951), 2026-05-05) | `verified` |
| 256-bit elliptic-curve cryptography needs under 1,200 to 1,450 logical and under 500,000 physical qubits, about 20 times lower than 2023.
