# Free learning resources and learning-science evidence, October 6, 2026

## Short version

- The best free material for customizing open models is live: the Hugging Face LLM Course, the AI Evals FAQ from Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar, Sebastian Raschka's repositories, the mlx-lm LoRA docs, and the Unsloth and OpenAI gpt-oss cookbooks.
- Several well-known resources are stale or unclear (the smol-course README, an old Gemma cookbook, one DeepLearning.AI course, Labonne's notebooks).
- Retrieval practice and spaced repetition are the best-supported study techniques for adults in general. Evidence for adults with ADHD is thin: the one study found, from 2023, showed no testing benefit.
- Public progress logging and "when X, I will do Y" planning have measurable effects on goal attainment, in studies that were not specific to ADHD.
- Many pages were read through a summarizing tool. Spot-check exact figures before reusing them.

Confidence words: `verified`, `likely`, `not verified`. See the [README](README.md). Each source is shown as ([label](link), date).

## 2. Learning-science evidence

**What is well supported for adults in general** (from samples that were not selected for ADHD):

- **Retrieval practice and spaced practice.** The conclusion is long established: Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) rated practice testing and distributed practice "high utility" and rereading, highlighting and summarization "low". The specific numbers could not be rechecked. The pages that held them were dead or blocked, so the figures usually quoted (Roediger and Karpicke 2006: 61 percent recall after a week for retrieval practice against 40 percent for rereading; Cepeda and colleagues 2006: 839 spacing assessments in 317 experiments) are `not verified`. `likely` for the general conclusion ([Roediger and Karpicke](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/), 2006; [APS summary of Dunlosky](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html), 2013-01).
- **Progress monitoring.** Harkin and colleagues (2016) reviewed 138 studies (N=19,951) and found monitoring progress raised goal attainment (d+ = 0.40, 95 percent interval 0.32 to 0.48), more so when progress was reported publicly or physically recorded. `verified` from the primary abstract ([White Rose repository](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91437/), 2016).
- **Implementation intentions** ("when X happens, I will do Y"). The 2006 Gollwitzer and Sheeran meta-analysis exists (Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38:69-119). The commonly quoted effect size of d = 0.65 over 94 tests could not be checked because the page showed no abstract. `verified` for the citation; `not verified` for the figure ([Konstanz](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes), 2006).

**Evidence for learners with attention differences is thin.**

- The one ADHD-specific retrieval study found: Stern and Halamish (Frontiers in Psychology, Nov 27, 2023) tested 72 university students (36 with ADHD, unmedicated) on short texts of 195 to 249 words. Neither retrieval condition significantly beat restudy, and recall organization was weaker in the ADHD group (p = 0.049). The authors list limits: short texts, a within-participants design, and unmedicated participants. A search-engine summary had implied a clear benefit, which the paper does not support. So retrieval practice is well supported in general but unproven for adults with ADHD in this setting. `verified` ([paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1301726/full), 2023-11-27).
- Direct trial evidence for body doubling (working alongside another person) and for ideal session length for adults with ADHD was not found. One press piece says no recent research tests body doubling, and another says accountability was the top reason students with ADHD gave for studying with another person. `not verified` ([Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/body-doubling-adhd), undated; [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/health/body-doubling-focus-benefits/), undated).
- Only the Stern and Halamish paper was read in full (and only the first part of it). The other learning-science citations came from search-result summaries.

**Streaks, games and abandonment** (general, not ADHD-specific):

- Duolingo's blog (Nov 19, 2020) reports that separating the goal from the streak raised day-14 retention by 3.3 percent, and a 10.5 percent rise in daily learners on a streak within 20 days. `verified` ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak), 2020-11-19).
- A 2012 efficacy study of Duolingo (Vesselinov and Grego) was commissioned by Duolingo. In 2021 its chief executive acknowledged limits on speaking, and TechCrunch called its pronunciation features "pretty buggy". Peer studies in search results say it builds recognition of vocabulary and grammar more than production. These were not rechecked. `likely` ([TechCrunch 2013](https://techcrunch.com/2013/01/17/study-learning-spanish-with-duolingo-can-be-more-effective-than-college-classes-or-rosetta-stone/amp), 2013-01-17; [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/03/duolingo-ec1-future/), 2021-05-03).
- Course abandonment is the norm. Jordan (2015) found a median completion rate of 12.6 percent across 221 online courses, though completion rates also rose over time. Epstein and colleagues (2016; 193 surveyed, 12 interviewed) found six reasons people stop tracking: collection burden, privacy, uncomfortable reflection, data quality, goal achieved, and life change. They also list guilt and frustration as feelings after abandonment. A claim of rates under 10 percent with no improvement over 6 years (Reich and Ruipérez-Valiente, Science, 2019), and a U-shaped engagement dip after about 4 weeks in gamification reviews, were not rechecked. `verified` for Jordan and Epstein ([Jordan](http://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/2112), 2015; [Epstein](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5428074), 2016); `likely` for the others ([MIT](https://tsl.mit.edu/research/the-mooc-pivot/), 2019-01).
- General reading of this evidence (an inference, not a sourced finding): lessons built around producing something, short entry points, a visible "goal reached" exit, and no guilt-inducing broken-streak displays fit the evidence better than recognition drills. `not verified`.
